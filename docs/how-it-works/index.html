How a DungeonFlux table works

A host sets up the table, friends join from their phones, anyone can chip in for the night, and the story is saved when you stop. Four steps, in this order.

Early access pilot This page describes the hosted pilot as designed. Features appear as they pass testing, and the rules page says what is supported.

  1. 1

    Invite your party

    The host signs in, creates a campaign, and picks the adventure, the rules and the number of players. Creating a campaign is free. The host then shares an invite link, or players type the room code shown on the TV.

    Each player signs in, picks a hero or brings one they saved earlier, and marks themselves ready.

    Good to know

    • A player can take a seat without paying anything when someone else sponsors the table.
    • Before joining, an invite shows only the campaign name, the host’s name and the content rating.
    • A room code finds a table. It does not let anyone take over another person’s seat.
    • The pilot is planned for adult groups, starting in the United States. Availability and age rules are still being confirmed.

  2. 2

    Fund the table and set a budget

    Play time and AI work are paid for with DungeonFlux service credit. New accounts will get $10 of promotional credit to try it. Anyone at the table can add more, and each person decides how much of their own credit to put toward tonight. Credit is never handed to the host.

    The host sets how much the table may spend per hour and for the whole session. Raising the table’s limit never raises what any one person has agreed to.

    Example: three people share an $8 session
    PersonPut inShare of $8Returned
    Host$20.00$4.00$16.00
    Player B$10.00$2.00$8.00
    Player C$10.00$2.00$8.00
    Total$40.00$8.00$32.00

    Illustrative example. Shares follow what each person authorised: 20, 10 and 10 gives 50%, 25% and 25%. Unused amounts go back to each person’s own credit, not to the host.

    Good to know

    • Nothing is charged for time in the lobby, before the host starts.
    • You can take back any part of your contribution that is not already held for work in progress.
    • Credit only becomes spendable after the payment is confirmed, not when you return from checkout.
    • Rates are on the pricing page. They are proposed and not final.

  3. 3

    Play on the TV and your phones

    The host links the TV to the table with a short code that works once and expires. The adventure plays on the shared screen. Private details, such as your character sheet and your choices, appear only on your own phone.

    Say what your hero does, or type it. The rules engine rolls the dice on the server, the TV shows roll results, and the Dungeon Master narrates what the dice decided. The host’s phone shows a small budget bar with the pot, anything held for work in progress, and the hourly limit.

    Good to know

    • The microphone is opt-in. Typed play works without it.
    • The TV can show the story but cannot spend credit or see private player information.
    • Pausing is free. While the table is paused, nothing accrues.
    • If everyone disconnects, the design is for new AI work and time billing to stop promptly.

  4. 4

    Save, settle and come back

    The screen says Saved only after your progress is safely stored. When the host ends the session, the clock stops, unused credit returns to the people who put it in, and each person can see a statement of what was used.

    Next time the same heroes and the same story resume where you left off. If the balance reaches zero, the table pauses and the campaign stays intact. Nothing is deleted because credit ran out.

    Good to know

    • You can export your own characters and the parts of a campaign you are allowed to see.
    • Restoring or branching a save never rewinds charges or refunds work already delivered.
    • Storage has limits. The proposed allowances are on the pricing page.

What the pilot covers

The first release is planned around one well-tested adventure or bounded free-roam experience, for two to six players depending on what testing supports. We will state the largest party we have actually tested on the rules page, and we will not claim more than that.

Subscriptions, automatic top-ups, cash transfers between players and public matchmaking are not part of the pilot.

Ready to try a table?

Sign-up opens with the early-access pilot. New accounts will get $10 of promotional credit, with no card needed to start.
